# Direct-sow sunflowers

**Question:** Which common sunflower seeds are hardy enough to grow from seed to flower when sown directly outdoors, without sprouting indoors first?

**Researched:** 26 November 2026

## Verdict

Yes — the common sunflower, *Helianthus annuus*, is one of the best plants for direct sowing outdoors in the UK, provided you wait until frost risk has passed and the soil has warmed. The RHS says annual seed can be sown outdoors in mid-April and May, while indoor sowing from late March is the more reliable route, not a requirement.

The word "hardy" needs care. Annual sunflowers are frost-tender and are killed by frost. In seed-trade language, "hardy annual" means the seed can be sown outdoors in spring. It does not mean the plant survives winter. Perennial sunflowers are genuinely winter-hardy, but they are not mainly a direct-sow crop: the RHS says they are normally bought as plants and propagated by division.

## When to sow in London

Direct sow mid-April to May in a warm, sheltered spot, once soil is at least 10°C. Early to mid-May is the safer, still-productive window, with succession sowings to early June for branching types.

In London, the last air frost had passed by 1 April in half of springs from 1996 to 2025, and by 14 April in nine springs in ten. The latest since 1950 was 6 May 1959. A first 20°C day was still followed by frost in 5 of the last 30 London springs, so check the forecast, not just the date.

Risks of sowing too early: seed rot and damping-off in cold, wet soil; frost killing emerged seedlings; slow, weak germination, leaving a long window for slug, mouse and bird damage. The RHS 2024 trial is direct evidence: very few direct sowings in early May 2024 succeeded in cold, wet weather and had to be replaced from glasshouse sowings.

Sowing too late is the opposite risk: giants may not flower before autumn. Gardeners' World gives 11 to 18 weeks to flower for annuals as a whole.

## Varieties

Days to flower are the least comparable figure in sunflower literature. Some sources give days to maturity, others days to first bloom, and US figures run faster than UK ones. Treat all day counts as plus or minus 2 to 3 weeks. The honest UK planning figure is about 70 to 120 days for giants, 50 to 75 days for dwarfs and fast single-stem cut types, and 60 to 95 days for branching mid-height types, with flowers in July to September from an April or May sowing.

No common *Helianthus annuus* variety was found described as "do not direct sow". Claims that sunflowers must be started indoors are about reliability in a cold, wet UK spring, not about the plant's biology.

Less suitable, or better started indoors, for risk or purpose rather than biology: giants grown for maximum height or competition; long-season giants sown late; expensive F1 single-stem cut-flower seed, where each lost seed is a lost stem; and modern series sold only as young or plug plants, where there is no seed to direct sow.

## Conditions for direct sowing

- **Soil temperature:** minimum 10°C at sowing depth. Best and fastest germination is 15–21°C, taking 7–10 days. Below 10°C, germination slows dramatically and seed may rot. Use a soil thermometer 5 cm deep, mid-morning, on consecutive days.
- **Sowing depth:** about 2 cm. Do not sow deeper than about 2.5 cm. Sow 2 seeds per station and thin to the strongest.
- **Spacing:** giants 45–60 cm apart; branching types 30–45 cm; dwarfs 20–30 cm. Dwarfs in pots: 1 plant in a 30 cm pot, 3 in a 40 cm pot.
- **Soil:** rich, fertile, moist but well-drained, with plenty of compost or well-rotted manure dug in. Mulch 5–7.5 cm after planting, kept off the stem. pH 6.0–7.5. Heavy clay reduces growth, so add compost or grit and avoid waterlogged hollows. *Helianthus debilis* is the exception: it tolerates poor, light, dry, even salty coastal soil.
- **Sun:** full sun, 6–8 or more hours direct sun daily, in a sheltered spot. Shade means leggy plants and small heads.
- **Watering:** annuals are thirsty; tall types must not dry out. Water well at sowing, keep moist until germination, then water deeply and regularly. Pots need daily watering in hot weather. Feed with potassium-rich tomato feed from bud stage.
- **Slugs:** the main UK threat. Slugs and snails love young annual sunflowers. Use a collar cut from a plastic bottle, copper tape, strulch, iron-phosphate pellets or traps, and nightly torch patrols for the first fortnight.
- **Birds, mice and squirrels:** eat seed before germination and seedlings after. Cover each station with a cloche, or use bird netting over the bed until plants outgrow the seedling stage.
- **Wind:** plant medium and tall types in a sheltered spot. Stake with a strong cane at planting time and tie loosely as the plant grows, re-tying at about 60 cm and 1.2 m. Giants over 2.4 m are best planted next to a house wall.

## Perennial sunflowers

Mainly no — this is overwhelmingly an annual-variety story for direct sowing. Perennial *Helianthus* are genuinely winter-hardy and return yearly, but the standard route is different: the RHS says they are sold as plants, as young plants in spring or as more mature plants in larger pots, and are propagated by dividing established clumps every 3 to 4 years.

Examples: Lemon Queen, perennial, 2 m, masses of small lemon-yellow flowers July to September; Dorian Roxburgh, 1–1.5 m, pale yellow, summer to autumn, propagate by seed or division; Anne, 1.5–2.5 m, seed indoors April to May or seed outdoors when frost risk has passed; *Helianthus maximiliani*, a tall clump-forming perennial; *Helianthus pauciflorus*, the exception, direct sow outdoors in autumn, self-sows freely.

**Naming trap:** Lemon Queen is sold both as an annual *Helianthus annuus* seed strain and as a perennial plant. Check the botanical name and whether you are buying seed or a potted plant.

Perennials typically take 1 to 2 years, some 2 to 5 years, to reach full size. If the goal is sow seed straight outdoors this spring and get flowers this summer, choose annuals.

## Best picks by use

- **Beginners:** Russian Giant, Teddy Bear, Sunspot, Big Smile, Autumn Beauty. Start with a dwarf and a giant to guarantee a result.
- **Small gardens and pots:** Teddy Bear, Sunspot, Choco Sun, Big Smile, Little Becka, Music Box, and RHS AGM dwarfs Desire Red, Waooh, Sunblast and Sunfinity. Dwarfs need no staking.
- **Tall giants:** Mongolian Giant, Russian Giant, American Giant, Titan or Giraffe, Kong. For maximum height, start indoors late March or early April, plant against a house wall, and water deeply twice a week. Direct-sown giants are still impressive but shorter and later.
- **Cutting:** Vanilla Ice, Autumn Beauty, Soraya, Velvet Queen, Music Box and Moulin Rouge for branching types; ProCut, Sunrich and Sunbright for single-stem pollen-free types. Succession-sow every 2 weeks.
- **Seeds and birds:** Russian Giant, Mammoth Grey Stripe, Mongolian Giant and Titan. Leave heads standing for finches, or bag the head once petals drop and cut when the back yellows. Double-flowered Teddy Bear and pollen-free hybrids are less useful for pollinators and seed.

## What we still do not know

- Per-variety days-to-flower in UK conditions. No UK replicated trial publishes days-to-bloom by variety for direct-sown plants.
- Little Becka height: UK guides say 30–40 cm; OSU says 3–4 ft. Unresolved; the UK figure is used above.
- Habit labels for Mammoth, Sunspot and Teddy Bear conflict between OSU and UK sources. Check packet descriptions at purchase.
- London last frost: 14 April in nine springs in ten is a grid statistic for air temperature at 2 m. Ground frost, frost hollows and the urban heat island mean a specific garden can differ by weeks.
- Sunfinity and Suncredible seed availability: some modern series are supplied as cuttings or plants, and whether a given series is sold as seed in the UK was not verified packet by packet.
